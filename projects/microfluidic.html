Microfluidic Membrane
Oxygenator

A PDMS-on-glass composite flat plate membrane device for blood gas exchange — designed, fabricated, and validated at the BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital.

Lab BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital
Device Type Flat Plate Membrane Oxygenator
Membrane PDMS-on-glass composite
CAD OnShape
Flow Cell 3D-printed polycarbonate

The microfluidic membrane oxygenator is a flat plate blood gas exchange device under development at the BRITE Lab at Nemours Children's Hospital. The device uses a PDMS-on-glass composite membrane as the gas transfer surface, with a 3D-printed polycarbonate flow cell that defines the blood-side channel geometry and provides structural support for the assembly.

My work on this project spans the full development cycle — from CAD modeling of the flow cell in OnShape through fabrication, assembly, and a rigorous verification and validation testing framework to characterize device performance and identify failure modes before progressing to biological testing.

Design & Development
Breakdown

01

Flow Cell Design & CAD

The polycarbonate flow cell was modeled in OnShape, defining the blood-side channel geometry, gasket seating surfaces, and fastener pattern. The design went through multiple iterations to optimize channel dimensions for target flow rates while maintaining structural integrity under clamping load. Viton cord stock is used as the channel-defining gasket and lateral seal, providing both geometry and compression sealing in a single component.

OnShape 3D Prototyping Polycarbonate Design Iteration
02

PDMS Membrane Fabrication

The gas transfer membrane is a thin PDMS film cast on a glass substrate, produced via spin coating followed by UV curing. PDMS provides high gas permeability for O₂ and CO₂ transfer while maintaining biocompatibility. The glass backing provides dimensional stability and a rigid bonding surface for assembly into the flow cell stack.

PDMS Spin Coating UV Curing Biomaterials
03

Fluid Dynamics Analysis

Channel geometry characterization uses fluid dynamic analysis to evaluate internal shear stress distributions and flow uniformity across the membrane surface. Ensuring physiologically safe shear stress levels is critical — excessive wall shear stress causes hemolysis, while insufficient flow leads to stagnation and poor gas transfer. The channel dimensions and flow rates are selected to maintain shear stress within the acceptable physiological window.

Fluid Dynamics Shear Stress Analysis Flow Uniformity
04

Verification & Validation Testing

A structured V&V protocol was developed to systematically characterize device integrity before advancing to performance testing. External leak detection uses a pressure hold protocol and submersion test to confirm housing seal integrity. Trans-membrane integrity is evaluated via dye perfusion — visual confirmation that the blood and gas compartments remain fully separated across the membrane. Bubble point testing characterizes membrane defect threshold pressure. Results are documented photographically at each stage.
