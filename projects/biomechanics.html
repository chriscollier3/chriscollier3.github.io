05 — Clinical Biomechanics

Squatting Biomechanics
Analysis

Lower extremity joint mechanics during squat patterns — kinematic and kinetic analysis using OpenCap motion capture with statistical evaluation in JMP.

Course Clinical Biomechanics
Motion Analysis OpenCap
Statistical Analysis JMP
Focus Joint Kinetics & Kinematics
Application Rehabilitation & Injury Prevention

The squat is one of the most fundamental movement patterns in both clinical rehabilitation and athletic performance contexts — and one of the most biomechanically complex. Joint loading at the knee, hip, and ankle during squatting varies significantly with technique, foot position, depth, and individual anatomy. Understanding these mechanics has direct implications for rehabilitation protocol design, injury prevention, and load management in clinical and athletic populations.

This project analyzed lower extremity joint mechanics during squat patterns using OpenCap, a markerless motion capture system, to collect kinematic data. Joint loading, muscle activation strategies, and kinematic variation across individuals were examined, with statistical analysis performed in JMP to evaluate significance of observed differences.

Analysis
Breakdown

01

OpenCap Motion Capture

OpenCap is a markerless motion capture system that uses smartphone cameras and computer vision to extract 3D body segment kinematics without the need for reflective markers or specialized lab equipment. Squat trials were recorded and processed through the OpenCap pipeline to generate joint angle time series and segment position data for analysis. The markerless approach reduces setup time and participant burden compared to traditional marker-based systems while maintaining sufficient accuracy for clinical biomechanics applications.

OpenCap Markerless Motion Capture 3D Kinematics Computer Vision
02

Joint Kinetics & Loading Analysis

Joint reaction forces and moments at the knee, hip, and ankle were estimated from kinematic data using inverse dynamics. Knee joint loading was a primary focus given its clinical relevance — patellofemoral compressive force and tibiofemoral shear force both vary substantially with squat depth and trunk lean angle. Analysis examined how individual technique differences translate to different joint loading profiles and what implications those differences carry for clinical populations with knee pathology.

Joint Kinetics Inverse Dynamics Knee Loading Patellofemoral Force
03

Statistical Analysis in JMP

Kinematic and kinetic outcomes were analyzed statistically in JMP to evaluate the significance of observed between-subject variation and technique differences. Descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, and visualization tools in JMP were used to characterize the distribution of joint angles and loading metrics across participants and identify variables that showed meaningful systematic differences across squat conditions.

JMP Biostatistics Hypothesis Testing Data Visualization
04

Rehabilitation & Clinical Implications

Findings were interpreted in the context of rehabilitation protocol design and injury prevention. The analysis examined how squat technique modifications — changes in depth, foot width, toe angle, and trunk position — alter joint loading in ways that are clinically meaningful for populations with knee osteoarthritis, ACL reconstruction, or patellofemoral pain. These insights connect quantitative biomechanical data to practical decisions about exercise prescription and load management in clinical and athletic settings.
