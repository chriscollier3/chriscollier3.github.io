06 — Senior Design

Dynamic Stability
Footwear Tester

A test fixture for an Instron materials testing machine that compresses a shoe while rolling it side to side, so footwear engineers can measure how strongly the shoe resists that roll. Ongoing team project, currently in fabrication.

Course Senior Capstone Design
Sponsor Under Armour
Status In Development

Footwear engineers need a repeatable way to measure how much a shoe resists rolling side to side while it carries weight. A standard compression test only pushes straight down, so it does not capture this.

The intended test applies vertical compression while producing controlled inversion and eversion, meaning side-to-side roll about the shoe’s heel-to-toe axis. The measurement goal is rotational stiffness: how much the resisting torque changes as the shoe rolls under a specified compressive load.

My Contributions

This is a team project. My involvement has focused on:

  • Mechanical concept development and SolidWorks CAD
  • Prototype fabrication, assembly, and troubleshooting
  • Researching and selecting gears, bearings, shaft hubs, and casting materials
  • Component procurement and cost tracking
  • Incorporating sponsor and advisor feedback into design iterations

Design & Development
Breakdown

01 — The Design Challenge

An Instron pushes and pulls along a vertical axis, and its rotary option rotates about that same vertical axis. The test needs compression from above while the shoe rolls about a horizontal heel-to-toe axis. The fixture has to convert one motion into the other, let the shoe compress freely, and stop the shoe from twisting or wandering in ways that would contaminate the torque reading.

02 — Two Initial Concepts

We explored two fixtures. A right-angle bevel gearbox redirects the Instron’s vertical-axis rotation into horizontal-axis rotation, rolling the shoe continuously. A locking universal joint holds the shoe at a fixed tilt so it can be compressed at set angles.

Concept Generation SolidWorks
Completed

03 — Concept Selection

We selected the gearbox for continued development. Fixed-angle compression gives snapshots, but rotational stiffness depends on how torque changes during the roll, which the gearbox can drive and measure directly.

Design Decisions Mechanical Design
Completed

04 — First Physical Prototype

We built the first gearbox prototype from a commercial gearbox, a wooden footbed, and a machined Instron attachment. Preliminary testing showed two problems: the shoe rotated about the vertical axis, and the rolling motion was inconsistent under compression.

When we held the shoe at the heel and toe by hand, it rolled the way we intended. That observation became the basis for the proposed heel/toe pin guides.

In Development

05 — Custom Gearbox

We are replacing the commercial gearbox with a custom-machined one built around purchased 1:1 steel miter gears (a matched pair of bevel gears that turn motion 90° without changing speed), ½-inch keyed steel shafts, and shaft-support bearings. A thrust bearing is included to carry the axial load from compression, so that load doesn’t pass through the gear teeth. Parts are currently being machined on the mills.

SolidWorks Machining Component Selection
Proposed

06 — Foot Insert & Heel/Toe Guides

On the prototype, the shafts were glued directly into wood. We have purchased shaft hubs to replace those joints, along with silicone molding material and casting resin to produce a contoured foot insert. Threaded inserts would secure the hubs to the cast insert.

The proposed heel/toe guides are meant to block unwanted vertical-axis rotation while still allowing vertical travel and inversion/eversion. Adapter details, guide geometry, and load paths are still under development.

Next Steps

07 — Fabrication & Verification

Once machining is finished, the next steps are to assemble the gearbox, cast the foot insert, and build the guides. Verification would then check that the fixture carries the required compressive load and produces clean roll without vertical-axis twist. It would also assess friction, backlash (play between gear teeth that shows up as lost motion when direction reverses), and repeatability across runs. None of these have been verified yet.

Verification Testing Assembly Repeatability

08 — Takeaway

Building and testing the rough prototype early exposed problems we had not anticipated. Holding the shoe by hand to see what motion it needed gave us the guide concept directly. The current design is driven by that observation rather than by assumptions about how the shoe would behave.
