04 — Pharmaceutical Modeling

Pharmacokinetic Modeling
of Propofol

A three-compartment computational simulation of Propofol pharmacokinetics built in MATLAB — modeling drug distribution, redistribution, and elimination dynamics following IV administration.

Course Pharmaceutical Modeling
Software MATLAB
Model Type Three-Compartment PK Model
Drug Propofol (IV anesthetic)
Method ODE System — Compartmental Analysis

Propofol is one of the most widely used intravenous anesthetics in clinical practice, valued for its rapid onset and short duration of action. Its pharmacokinetic behavior is well-described by a three-compartment model — a central plasma compartment connected to a rapidly-equilibrating peripheral compartment and a slowly-equilibrating deep tissue compartment. Understanding the kinetics across these compartments is essential for predicting plasma concentration over time and maintaining therapeutic dosing without toxicity.

This project built a full three-compartment PK simulation of Propofol from first principles in MATLAB, using a system of ordinary differential equations to model drug transfer between compartments and elimination from plasma. The simulation produces plasma concentration-time profiles that capture the characteristic rapid redistribution phase and slower elimination phase seen clinically after IV bolus administration.

Model Design &
Implementation

01

Three-Compartment Model Structure

The model consists of three compartments connected by first-order rate constants: a central compartment (plasma and highly perfused tissues), a peripheral compartment (moderately perfused tissues), and a deep compartment (slowly perfused tissues like fat and muscle). Drug is administered as an IV bolus into the central compartment and eliminated from the central compartment via hepatic metabolism and renal excretion. Transfer between compartments is governed by intercompartmental clearance rate constants k12, k21, k13, and k31.

Three-Compartment Model Compartmental Analysis Rate Constants IV Bolus
02

ODE System & MATLAB Implementation

The compartmental model translates directly into a system of three coupled ordinary differential equations describing the rate of change of drug mass in each compartment over time. The system was implemented in MATLAB using ode45 for numerical integration, with literature-validated pharmacokinetic parameters for Propofol (volumes of distribution, clearance values, and intercompartmental rate constants). Initial conditions reflect drug mass immediately after IV bolus injection.

MATLAB ODE System ode45 Numerical Integration
03

Concentration-Time Profile Analysis

Simulation outputs were converted from drug mass to plasma concentration using compartment volumes, producing the characteristic biphasic concentration-time curve seen clinically with Propofol. The initial steep decline reflects rapid redistribution from plasma into peripheral tissues, while the slower terminal phase reflects hepatic elimination. The simulation illustrates the narrow window between therapeutic plasma concentrations and toxic levels, highlighting why continuous infusion with dose adjustment is used for prolonged anesthesia.

04

Clinical Relevance & Interpretation

The model demonstrates why Propofol's clinical behavior — rapid induction, short duration of action after a single bolus, and accumulation during prolonged infusion — emerges directly from its compartmental pharmacokinetics. Context-sensitive half-time, the time for plasma concentration to fall by 50% after stopping an infusion, increases with infusion duration as peripheral compartments saturate and return drug to plasma. These insights connect mathematical modeling directly to clinical anesthesia practice.
