Hollow Fiber Membrane
Oxygenator

A novel bonding system for stacked Oxyplus PMP hollow fiber membrane sheets — developed through systematic approach elimination, robotic UV adhesive dispensing, and custom tooling design at the BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital.

Lab BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital
Membrane Oxyplus PMP 90/200 (3M Membrana)
Bonding Method UV Adhesive Robotic Dispensing
Dispensing Robot Nordson EFD E3V
Adhesive Panacol Vitralit UV
CAD OnShape
Prototyping FDM 3D Printing (Bambu X1 Carbon)
Status Mock sheet validation complete, Oxyplus sheets pending

The hollow fiber membrane oxygenator project involves developing a novel bonding system for stacking sheets of Oxyplus PMP 90/200 hollow fiber membranes from 3M Membrana into a functional multi-layer oxygenator. PMP (polymethylpentene) is the industry standard material for high-performance oxygenator membranes due to its excellent gas permeability, low plasma leakage resistance, and established biocompatibility profile.

The core engineering challenge is sealing stacked fiber sheets together without causing corner flow stasis — a known thrombosis risk in rectangular membrane geometries — while ensuring bonds are verifiable layer by layer and that no adhesive wicks into the fiber lumens. My work involved systematically evaluating and eliminating candidate bonding approaches, then developing and validating an automated UV adhesive dispensing system using a Nordson EFD E3V robot to deposit a precise circular adhesive ring on each sheet, eliminating corner stasis by design. Bond strength was validated on PLA mock sheets, with substrate-level failure observed on peel testing — indicating adhesive bond strength exceeding the substrate material.

Design & Development
Breakdown

01

Problem Definition & Approach Elimination

Before committing to any bonding strategy, candidate approaches were systematically evaluated and eliminated against a defined set of constraints: no axial fiber compression (which crushes lumens and eliminates CO2 transfer, per Heyer et al. 2025), no unverifiable interior bonds, no leachable materials in the blood path, and a circular/elliptical blood flow boundary to eliminate corner stasis. Approaches ruled out included adhesive transfer tape (leaching risk), mechanical gasket compression (fiber crushing), TPU printed rings (no adhesion to PMP-like surfaces), and edge potting (no flow boundary geometry control).

02

Robotic UV Adhesive Dispensing System

The validated approach uses a Nordson EFD E3V automated dispensing robot programmed in its proprietary SRC command language to deposit a precise circular bead of Panacol Vitralit UV-curable adhesive on each sheet. The circular bead geometry eliminates corner stasis by design — the adhesive ring defines a circular blood flow channel, with the sheet corners sitting outside the active flow path. The full program automates dispensing, two-stage UV cure sweeps, layer wait periods, and Z-axis stepping for each successive sheet, requiring only manual sheet placement between cycles. Bond strength on PLA mock sheets exceeded substrate strength on peel testing.

Nordson EFD E3V SRC Programming UV Adhesive Panacol Vitralit Automation
03

UV Cure Protocol Development

UV adhesive curing required developing a precise two-stage protocol. A fast initial sweep (175 mm/s) gels the adhesive to a tacky state without over-curing the surface — over-curing eliminates surface reactivity and prevents the next sheet from bonding. A second sweep after sheet placement and compression locks the sheet before a 30-second full cure at a fixed position directly above the sheet. Ambient light degradation of the adhesive in the syringe was identified as a key process variable and mitigated by wrapping the syringe in aluminum foil from the point of loading. PSI and sweep speed parameters were systematically optimized through experimental iteration on mock sheets.

04

UV Pen Mount Design

A custom mount was designed in OnShape and printed on the Bambu X1 Carbon to hold the UWAVE UTARGET-365 UV pen on the Nordson EFD E3V Z-axis carriage. The mount design had to accommodate the pen's tapered body geometry — wider at the emitter end and narrower toward the top — requiring a stepped bore and set screw to lock the pen in place without it slipping through under gravity. The CL04 optical head was also identified and quoted from UWAVE to reduce the beam spot from 7-9mm to 2-3mm at 8-10mm working distance, enabling more precise ring-width curing in future iterations.
