02 — Engineering Research & Development

Hollow Fiber Membrane
Oxygenator

A scalable oxygenator built from stacked Oxyplus PMP hollow fiber membrane sheets — developed through systematic design iteration, 3D prototyping, and centrifugal potting at the BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital.

Lab BRITE Lab, Nemours Children's Hospital
Membrane Oxyplus PMP 90/200 (3M Membrana)
Potting Method Centrifugal, bent fiber geometry
CAD OnShape
Prototyping FDM 3D Printing

The hollow fiber membrane oxygenator project involves the design and development of a scalable gas exchange device using stacked sheets of Oxyplus PMP hollow fiber membranes from 3M Membrana. PMP (polymethylpentene) is the industry standard material for high-performance oxygenator membranes due to its excellent gas permeability, low plasma leakage resistance, and established biocompatibility profile.

The core engineering challenge is potting — the process of sealing the fiber bundle ends to create separated blood and gas compartments while preserving every fiber lumen. My work involved systematically evaluating bonding and sealing strategies before converging on a centrifugal potting approach with bent fiber geometry, a solution independently developed and subsequently validated against established industrial methods in the literature.

Design & Development
Breakdown

01

Device Geometry & CAD Modeling

Iterative 3D CAD modeling in OnShape was used to develop and refine the device housing geometry, fiber stack arrangement, and potting chamber design. Physical prototypes were printed in FDM using polycarbonate and polypropylene to test assembly fit, fiber alignment, and potting compound fill behavior before committing to final geometry. The design went through multiple major revisions as potting approach requirements evolved.

OnShape 3D Printing Design Iteration Polycarbonate
02

Potting Strategy Development

Potting is the most technically demanding aspect of HFM oxygenator fabrication. The potting compound must seal every fiber end, bond to the housing, and set without wicking into fiber lumens — which would permanently block gas transfer. Several bonding and sealing approaches were systematically evaluated and eliminated based on practical constraints including axial fiber compression, unverifiable interior bonds, and potential for blood-contacting leachables.

Potting Adhesive Evaluation Biomaterials Systematic Testing
03

Centrifugal Potting with Bent Fiber Geometry

The final approach uses centrifugal force to drive potting compound into the fiber bundle ends while the device spins. The fibers are arranged in a bent geometry — curving away from the potting face — which eliminates corner flow stasis zones in the blood path and produces a curved open flow channel through the fiber bundle. This solution preserves fiber lumen integrity during potting and was independently converged upon before being validated against centrifugal potting methods documented in the HFM oxygenator literature for Oxyplus PMP fibers specifically.

Centrifugal Potting Bent Fiber Geometry Flow Path Design Literature Validation
04

Mock Material Testing

PMP is not a printable material, making direct prototype fabrication impossible. Polypropylene filament was identified as the closest printable analog to PMP based on surface energy characteristics — both are low-surface-energy polyolefins that present similar adhesive bonding challenges. Mock material testing on polypropylene prints allows adhesive candidates and bonding protocols to be evaluated before committing to actual Oxyplus membrane material.
